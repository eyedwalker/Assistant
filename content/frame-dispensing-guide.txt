Frame Dispensing and Fitting Guide

Summary: Professional guide for eyeglass frame selection, fitting, and adjustment techniques to ensure optimal comfort, appearance, and vision correction for patients.

Key Learning Objectives:
- Master frame measurement and fitting techniques
- Understand different frame materials and their properties
- Perform accurate lens measurements and calculations
- Execute professional frame adjustments and repairs
- Provide excellent customer service during the dispensing process

Frame Selection Consultation:
Patient lifestyle assessment determines appropriate frame styles and materials. Face shape analysis guides frame selection for optimal appearance and balance. Prescription considerations affect lens thickness and frame compatibility. Budget discussions ensure realistic expectations and appropriate product recommendations.

Frame Measurements and Sizing:
Eye size measurement represents the horizontal lens width in millimeters. Bridge size determines the distance between lenses and nose fit. Temple length affects behind-ear comfort and stability. Frame width calculation ensures proper fit across the face. Vertex distance impacts prescription accuracy, particularly for higher powers.

Progressive Lens Fitting:
Fitting height measurement determines the position of the distance zone in progressive lenses. Pantoscopic tilt affects the lens power curve and visual zones. Frame wrap angle influences peripheral vision and lens power distribution. Pupillary distance accuracy ensures proper optical center alignment.

Frame Materials and Properties:
Metal frames offer durability and adjustability with materials including titanium, stainless steel, and memory metals. Plastic frames provide lightweight comfort with options including acetate, nylon, and TR-90. Hybrid designs combine metal and plastic for optimal performance. Hypoallergenic materials accommodate patients with metal sensitivities.

Lens Considerations:
High-index materials reduce lens thickness for strong prescriptions. Polycarbonate lenses provide impact resistance for safety applications. Trivex offers excellent optical clarity with impact protection. Anti-reflective coatings improve vision quality and appearance.

Frame Adjustments and Repairs:
Temple adjustments address slipping and comfort issues behind the ears. Nose pad modifications improve weight distribution and prevent sliding. Bridge adjustments accommodate different nose shapes and widths. Emergency repairs include temple tightening, nose pad replacement, and minor frame straightening.

Quality Control and Verification:
Lens power verification confirms accurate prescription filling. Optical center measurement ensures proper alignment with pupillary distance. Frame inspection identifies defects or damage before dispensing. Final fitting assessment includes comfort, appearance, and vision quality.

Patient Education and Care Instructions:
Proper cleaning techniques preserve lens coatings and frame finishes. Storage recommendations prevent damage and maintain frame adjustments. Handling instructions reduce risk of breakage and misalignment. Warranty information covers manufacturing defects and normal wear limitations.

Troubleshooting Common Issues:
Sliding frames may require nose pad adjustment or temple modification. Lens distortion can result from frame warping or improper fitting. Pressure points indicate need for frame adjustment or size changes. Vision problems may require prescription verification or fitting measurements.

Specialized Fitting Situations:
Children's frames require durable materials and secure fitting. Safety glasses need impact-resistant lenses and secure retention. Computer glasses benefit from anti-reflective coatings and blue light filtering. Sports frames require secure fit and impact protection.

Digital Integration:
Frame styling apps help patients visualize different options. Digital measuring devices improve fitting accuracy. Inventory management systems track frame availability and reorder needs. Patient records maintain fitting preferences and adjustment history.

Sales Techniques and Customer Service:
Consultation skills identify patient needs and preferences. Product knowledge enables confident recommendations. Objection handling addresses cost and styling concerns. Follow-up service ensures satisfaction and builds long-term relationships.

Professional Development:
Training programs update skills in new products and techniques. Certification courses validate professional competence. Industry events provide networking and education opportunities. Manufacturer training ensures product expertise and warranty compliance.
